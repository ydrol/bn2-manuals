Getting started

The timer controls when the water heats, so setting it to suit your schedule and requirements is the priority function of this guide. Using the timer also helps you to manage your electricity usage.

The tank needs around two hours to heat fully from cold, so set each time to start about two hours before you usually need hot water, for example set timer for 6am for morning showers required around 8am.

Once heated, the tank stays hot for several hours, so there is no need to leave the heater on all day. Heat the water two hours before you first need it, and switch off for longer periods when it is not needed (e.g. when at work).

If you need extra hot water outside the set times, press the large square button on the front, which is BOOST. It is not instant: the tank still needs time to heat.

To view or set the timer, use the small buttons behind the front panel.

To open the panel, hook a fingernail under its top edge and pull it towards you and down. It hinges at the bottom and hangs open, and the display stays on the unit. Do not touch the two screws either side of the buttons, and do not lever at the bottom of the outer white plate, because that is where the electrician's wiring is.

With the panel open, you will see two buttons in the middle and a row of three below them.

ButtonWhat it does

Use the 24-hour clock to set times, e.g. 15.00 for 3pm and 17.00 for 5pm. Holding − or + changes the number quickly.

What times is it set to?

You can look through the saved times without changing anything, as long as you only press SET and never touch − or +.

  1. Open the front panel.
  2. Press SET (short presses) until the display shows 01 and ON beside a time. On the way it passes through the clock settings; SET alone leaves them unchanged.
  3. Write down the time shown. That is when the heater first comes on.
  4. Keep pressing SET. The display moves to 01 OFF (first off time), then 02 ON, 02 OFF, and so on. Each time takes two presses, one for the hours and one for the minutes.
  5. When a period shows --:00, nothing more is set and you have seen the whole schedule.
  6. Press and hold SET until the normal clock display returns.

If you are not sure whether weekdays and weekends have separate times, the next section shows how to tell.

Check the daily program (same every day, weekday/weekend or each day separately)

The timer runs on one of three programs, which the electrician chose when it was installed. You can check which one without changing anything.

  1. Open the front panel.
  2. Press SET (short presses) until the display shows 01 and ON.
  3. Look at which day names are lit along the top, and compare them with the pictures below.
  4. Press and hold SET to go back to the normal display. As long as you have not pressed − or +, nothing has changed.

Only the landlord can change the program as it requires resetting and setting up the timer again. If you have different requirements such as separate times at weekends, inform the landlord which program it is currently on and what you would prefer.

To change the program on/off times

As stated earlier, the water takes about two hours to heat from cold, and once heated stays hot for several hours.

You can set up to 10 heating periods a day, so you do not need one continuous ‘on’ time. A few shorter periods, each starting about two hours before you usually need hot water (for example before morning showers and before the evening), will give you hot water when you want it while keeping your electricity usage down.

This example sets hot water from 6am to 8:30am and from 15.00 to 17.00 (3pm to 5pm), every day. Whatever is flashing is what − and + will change, and SET moves you on to the next thing.

The water is cold and you need it now

BOOST will start heating the water immediately, but it may not be hot straight away. The tank takes about two hours to heat fully from cold, and you will get some warm water sooner.

Press the large square button on the front to BOOST. You do not need to open the panel, and the button still works when the keypad is locked.

Ideally, set the timer so the water is already hot whenever you usually need it, rather than waiting on a Boost. When the Boost ends, the timer goes back to its normal times by itself, so the schedule is not affected. If you find yourself boosting often, adjust the timer to heat according to your schedule.

The heater is on a scheduled time but you want to switch it off just this once

If the heater is already on during a scheduled time and you want it off, press BOOST once. It stays off until the next scheduled on time.

Going away

Open the front panel and press OFF/ON once. The display shows OFF, and the heater will not come on at its scheduled times. Your saved times are kept.

When you get back, press OFF/ON again and the normal display returns. If you need hot water while the timer is paused, BOOST still works.

Changing the clocks for spring (forward) or winter (back)

The timer does not change for summer and winter time by itself, but you do not need to set your times again. One button moves the clock, and your on and off times move with it, so 6am still means 6am.

  1. On the last Sunday in March (clocks go forward) or the last Sunday in October (clocks go back), make sure the normal clock is showing.
  2. Press and hold BOOST until the clock moves by one hour.
  3. Check the clock is now right. If it moved the wrong way, press and hold BOOST again.

A short press of BOOST starts a boost instead, and the display shows 0:30. If that happens, press BOOST three more times until it shows 0:00, then try again, holding it for longer.

If the clock is wrong by some other amount, open the panel, then press and hold SET from the normal display. Use − or + to correct whatever is flashing (day, then hours, then minutes), pressing SET after each, then press and hold SET to save.
